01 · The problem

You run the cars.
The juggling runs you.

If you dispatch a handful of cabs out of Andheri or run a tours-and-travels desk in Pune, nobody handed you an operations system. Your board is a paper diary, a WhatsApp group, and your own memory — and at rush hour all three break down at once.

🗒️

The diary can't tell you who's free

A new booking comes in and you flip pages to work out which driver and which car are actually free at 6pm. Sometimes you double-book the same Innova.

💬

WhatsApp is not a dispatch board

Trip details, driver replies and "reached, sir" pings are scattered across a group. By afternoon nobody can say what's still unassigned or ongoing.

💸

Advance in, balance forgotten

The advance was taken at booking; the balance is meant to be collected at drop-off. Some drivers collect, some forget, and the ledger is a scrap of paper.

🧾

"Sir, how much to collect?"

The driver calls from the road to ask the fare and the balance. Without a duty slip in his hand, the amount at drop-off is whatever he remembers.

02 · The insight

The bookings are known a day ahead — same cars, same drivers, a fare on every trip. Only the juggling is manual, and it's the juggling that drops the ball.

SafarBook's whole job is to put that juggling on one screen: book the trip, assign the driver and car, track the advance and balance, print the duty slip — so at 7am the board shows every car out and every rupee tracked, without anyone reconstructing it from WhatsApp.

03 · How it works

Book it. Assign it. Collect it. The board keeps score.

  1. 1

    Book a trip

    Customer, from → to, date, type (local / outstation / airport / rental), fare and advance. A booking confirmation is queued to the customer the moment it's saved.

  2. 2

    Assign a driver + vehicle

    Attach a car and driver from your fleet. The trip moves to assigned on the board, and each driver's load for the day is visible at a glance.

  3. 3

    Track advance / balance, print the duty slip

    Record money as it comes in; the balance falls automatically. Print a numbered duty slip — route, guest and exactly how much to collect — and it's queued to the driver.

  4. 4

    Work the daily dispatch board

    Today's trips, the needs-a-driver queue, ongoing duties, completed today, and every pending balance across the book — one screen, always current.

07 · Who it's for & questions

Built for the local operator, not the aggregator.

SafarBook is for the operator with a handful of drivers and cars — a cab fleet in Andheri, a tours-and-travels desk in Pune, an airport-run operation with five Innovas. Big enough that the juggling eats your mornings; small enough that a fleet-management suite is overkill. If you run it on a diary and a WhatsApp group today, this is for you.

Taxi & cab operators Tours & travels desks Airport-run fleets 3–30 vehicles
Does it send the booking confirmation and duty slip itself?

SafarBook drafts every message — the customer's booking confirmation, the payment note when money comes in, and the driver's duty slip — and stages it in your outbox. You send from your own number with a tap, so it always looks like it came from your office, not a robot.

We take cash advances and UPI on the way back. Does that work?

Yes. Record an advance when the booking is made and record the balance when the driver collects it — cash or UPI, doesn't matter. SafarBook keeps the balance = fare minus advance up to date, so you always know exactly what each driver has to collect.

What is a duty slip and does the driver need it?

A duty slip is the numbered paper the driver carries for a trip: the guest, the route, the trip type, and exactly how much balance to collect. SafarBook prints it from the trip detail and queues a copy to the driver, so nobody argues about the amount at drop-off.

How long does setup take?

An afternoon. You add your drivers and vehicles once, then start booking trips. The dispatch board is populated from day one — today's trips, who still needs a driver, what's ongoing, and every pending balance across the book.

What does it cost?

Pricing is worked out on a quick discovery call and billed monthly on subscription. We're keeping public prices off the page while we onboard our first operators.
